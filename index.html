US hours of service trip planner with day grids that start today, four clocks, limit warnings, an automatic trip planner and a split sleeper toggle

Duty status grid. Left and right arrows move between 30 minute segments. Up and down arrows change the status of the segment. Keys 1 to 4 set off duty, sleeper berth, driving, or on duty. Hold shift while moving to paint the status across segments. Home and End jump to the start or end of the day. Page up and page down move one hour. Enter sets the clocks to this time. To move the clocks with a pointer, drag the red marker at the top of a grid.

Pick a start and how much you need to drive. The plan fills your grids with on-duty time, driving, 30-minute breaks, and rest that stay inside the limits, and adds days when needed. It uses anything already in your grids before the start time, counts the cycle hours you have left, and follows the Split sleeper switch.
Cycle left is the 70-hour cycle time you have at the start of today, before anything in the grids.
Clocks as of
Tap or drag on a row to set a 30 minute block. To move the clocks, drag the red marker at the top of a grid or tap the hour ruler. Red means driving over a limit. Today starts rested (10 hours or more off); if you're mid-shift, mark the hours already worked. With a keyboard, use arrow keys to move, 1 to 4 to set the status, and Enter to move the clocks here.

Planning tool only

This is not a certified electronic logging device (ELD), and it isn't legal or compliance advice. It models simplified US federal rules for property-carrying drivers: the 11-hour driving limit, the 14-hour window, the 30-minute break, the 70-hour/8-day cycle with a basic 34-hour restart, and a simplified split sleeper pair.

Miles are estimated from your average speed and driving time, not from GPS or an odometer. It doesn't cover adverse driving conditions, short-haul exceptions, personal conveyance, yard moves, or state and carrier rules. Trip plans are suggestions, not a guarantee of compliance, and results can differ from your official logs.

Always follow the current FMCSA regulations (49 CFR Part 395) and your carrier's policies.
